In Euclidean geometry the perpendicular distance between the rays remains equal to the distance from P to Q as we move to the right.
However, in the early nineteenth century two alternative geometries were proposed. In hyperbolic geometry (from the Greek hyperballein, "to exceed") the distance between the rays increases. In elliptic geometry (from the Greek elleipein, "to fall short") the distance decreases and the rays eventually meet. These non-Euclidean geometries were later incorporated in a much more general geometry developed by C. F. Gauss and G. F. B. Riemann (it is this more general geometry that is used in Einstein's general theory of relativity).
